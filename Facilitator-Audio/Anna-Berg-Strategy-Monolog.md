## Section 2 — Q1 numbers

Q1 was tough. Our total logistics cost was three point eight percentage points worse than the Q4 baseline. In absolute terms, that's about three hundred and twenty thousand euros of cost we did not budget for. None of that is structural — it's three discrete issues, and I want to walk you through each one because we'll be questioned on this next week.

The headline is: we are not happy with the result, but we know exactly what happened, we know who's owning each item, and two of the three issues are already contained.

Before I go into the drivers, one note on the bigger picture. The Kuehne and Nagel relationship is healthy. They are absorbing most of the financial impact themselves — they have not asked us to share the pain, and I want to acknowledge that.

That said, we need to use this moment to push for the improvements we've been waiting on. The cold-chain visibility solution they committed to at the last QBR is now overdue. I'll come back to that.

OK — the three drivers.

---

## Section 3 — The three drivers

Driver one. Hamburg port demurrage on our reefer containers going to Singapore. Fourteen containers stuck at the port between four and nine days each. Root cause: the new MRN customs format introduced in mid-January — Kuehne and Nagel's documentation template did not match the new schema. They fixed it by mid-February. No further excursions since. The disputed amount is around thirty-eight thousand euros, and Kuehne and Nagel is fighting that with the port authority directly. I expect a goodwill split, but I want a date.

Sandra, please ask Markus Hoffmann for a written commitment on when this case closes. End of May at the latest.

Driver two. The Frankfurt cold-chain incident. Batch CTI-X48, three pallets above eight degrees for ninety minutes. The chiller at the cross-dock failed. Notional product value about a hundred and ten thousand euros. Their liability cap per shipment is twenty-five thousand. We have a write-off claim under review.

This one stings the most. Not the money — the reputation. We have just signed a new three-year pharma contract, and if the customer hears that our Frankfurt cold-chain handling failed in Q1, we have a problem. I want a proactive narrative ready before our pharma customer asks.

Driver three. APAC user provisioning. Five weeks where our Singapore and Shanghai teams couldn't log into the Kuehne and Nagel portal. That's on us — it was our Microsoft licensing issue. Hiroshi, I'll come back to you on this.

## Section 5 — Wrocław and the new pharma contract

Wrocław. The plant commissions in Q3. Initial capacity twenty-four thousand tonnes per year, ramping over eighteen months. For logistics, two things change. We shift roughly a hundred and twenty TEU per year out of our APAC sea inbound — those are polymer additives we used to import from China and Vietnam. And we start having significant intra-EU finished goods flows out of Poland to our German and Dutch customers.

The APAC reduction is good news for our margin profile. The intra-EU flow is a question mark — we have not decided yet whether to push that through Kuehne and Nagel's road network or to put it out to a contract logistics tender. I want to keep that decision open until July.

Sandra, please get a baseline volume forecast from the Wrocław commissioning team by mid-June. I need it for the July call.

The pharma contract. Three years, two hundred and twenty million euros, niche oncology intermediates. Volume hits Frankfurt cold-chain from Q3. Forty percent uplift on that lane. The customer is a top-five European pharma player and they will audit our logistics handling. I cannot stress this enough — Frankfurt has to be airtight by August.

---

## Section 6 — Scope 3 mandate

Scope 3. Eva Lindqvist signed off the updated ESG roadmap in March. The relevant headlines for our department.

By Q4 of this year, our logistics partners must report shipment-level CO₂e data. Not annual averages. Not modelled estimates. Per-shipment. By Q4.

By 2027, all air freight has to be at least ten percent sustainable aviation fuel blended. We accept Book-and-Claim if it's certified by ISCC or RSB.

And Eva said this in her Chemical Industry Weekly interview last month, in writing: we are willing to pay a premium for resilience, we are not willing to pay a premium for marketing claims. End quote. That means our 3PLs need to deliver the data, the certificates, and the proof — not slides with green leaves on them.

Kuehne and Nagel can do shipment-level CO₂e — they have the data model. Whether they can deliver it to our SAP feed by Q4 of this year is a different question. That's what I want to test next Wednesday.

Hiroshi, can you check whether their APAC operation is on the same data platform as their European operation? If not, we have an integration problem.
